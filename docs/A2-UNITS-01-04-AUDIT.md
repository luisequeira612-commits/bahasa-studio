# A2 editorial audit — units 1–4

**Status:** internal editorial/structural review; not native-speaker validation  
**Scope:** `curriculum-a2.js`, units `a2_u1`–`a2_u4`  
**Date:** 2026-10-31

## Method and limits

This pass checks the authored curriculum data for alignment between unit goals, vocabulary, lesson sequencing, prompts, model answers, and translations. It also checks whether the learner is given enough context to interpret the target structure.

It does **not** certify Indonesian naturalness, register, regional usage, CEFR alignment, or translation equivalence. No changes are made to learner-facing Indonesian content in this pass because those judgments need a qualified language reviewer. The findings below are editorial questions or coverage gaps, not claims that a phrase is ungrammatical.

## Unit-by-unit notes

### A2-U1 — Días, meses y fechas

- **What is in place:** all seven weekdays and twelve months are represented; the five lessons sequence weekdays, dates, and months; practice includes recognition, translation, listening, ordering, and guided writing.
- **Coverage gap:** the unit description promises “fechas sencillas”, but the visible practice primarily asks for the weekday or month. There is no explicit worked example for expressing a calendar date (for example, a day number together with a month), nor a guided exercise that asks the learner to produce one.
- **Editorial follow-up:** add a carefully checked date-format example and a guided production item once the exact Indonesian convention and punctuation have been reviewed. Keep this separate from the existing weekday/month drills.

### A2-U2 — Expresar cuándo ocurre una acción

- **What is in place:** the unit introduces `sudah`, `belum`, `sedang`, and `akan`, then broadens to recent-time expressions, continuity, and past/future time phrases. The explanations correctly foreground that Indonesian verbs do not conjugate for grammatical tense.
- **Teaching opportunity:** `belum` (“not yet”) and `masih` (“still”) are both introduced, but their contrast is not directly tested in a paired context. A short contrastive item would help learners distinguish an action that has not happened from a state that continues.
- **Editorial follow-up:** add a minimal-pair exercise only after checking that the chosen context makes the distinction unambiguous.

### A2-U3 — Ordenar acontecimientos y contar secuencias

- **What is in place:** the four lessons progress from basic sequence markers to “before”, “next”, “when”, duration, and endpoint. The exercises include translation, listening, ordering, and writing.
- **Context/translation ambiguity:** several vocabulary examples are translated into Spanish in the present (“Primero, como”; “Luego, bebo agua”; “Finalmente, regreso a casa”), while the unit description invites learners to narrate events and the grammar note mentions the past. Since Indonesian verb forms do not themselves mark tense, this is not necessarily a language error; however, the intended timeline is underspecified.
- **Editorial follow-up:** state explicitly that the example sentences are interpreted through context and can describe a routine or a past sequence; add a short paired context showing how the same verb form is understood differently. Do not mechanically change the Indonesian verbs to create a “past tense”.

### A2-U4 — Narrar una jornada cotidiana

- **What is in place:** vocabulary and lesson objectives align around routines, schedules, start/end times, activities, and describing a busy or relaxed day. Prior A1 routine vocabulary is deliberately included as review.
- **Teaching opportunity:** time expressions use examples such as `pukul delapan` and `pukul dua belas`; learners would benefit from one explicit reminder that `pukul` introduces clock time, plus a short learner-generated schedule that combines at least two activities and their times.
- **Editorial follow-up:** make the schedule task integrate the vocabulary already taught rather than adding new grammar or changing the curriculum identifiers.

## Cross-unit checks

- The four units retain their existing unit and vocabulary IDs.
- Lessons reference canonical vocabulary IDs; Unit 4 also intentionally references A1 review vocabulary.
- The authored lesson structures include varied exercise types, listening, and guided writing.
- No Indonesian wording, translations, assessment answers, SRS data, progress records, backup formats, or curriculum IDs were modified by this audit.
- No claim of native-speaker validation is made.
